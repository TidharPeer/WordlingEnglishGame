# WORDLINGS — The Lost Worlds

## Vision and visual bar
An original cozy mystery adventure: understanding English changes a place, helps a friend and reveals the next clue. Compare rendered screens against the strongest supplied fantasy adventure references for composition, layered landscapes, warm light and expressive silhouettes. Evaluate functional quality by completing a saved journey with deliberate mistakes.

Midnight teal shadows, jade canopies, pale mint paths, amber lanterns and lavender crystal light. Custom SVG environments use rounded silhouettes, foreground framing and layered depth. Explorer equipment inspires dark rounded panels, luminous edges and cream text. English and Hebrew remain readable, with English challenges always LTR. Floating, blinking and celebration animations have reduced-motion alternatives. No emoji substitute for final character artwork.

Three companion silhouettes: Pip, an energetic pocket-sized fox; Moss, a patient leaf-eared forest creature; Luma, a curious lantern sprite. Cosmetics never change learning performance. Mistakes never harm a companion.

## First five minutes
The title reveals a broken crystal over a living forest. Choose language, nickname, avatar and companion without an account. Puck has delivered a letter to a mushroom because signs forgot their words. Find an apple and fox, greet Puck, assemble a short sentence and hear a familiar word. Each response earns feedback and progress. Completing the signpost reveals a blue feather, opens the trail and rewards a home decoration. These interactions sample several skills without an exam or ability label.

## World 1 and cast
Six chapters with six authored challenges each provide several short resumable sessions. Dialogue choices can yield distinct character reactions without punishing a valid alternative. Bramble joins the companion collection after the bridge chapter. Wake a signpost, solve Nettle’s picnic confusion, hear Hush’s echo trees, fix Tock’s lanterns, help Bramble at the bridge and find Lyra guarding the fragment. The disappearance resolves; a mechanical messenger reveals a deliberate mystery behind the broken crystal.

Puck is an earnest courier. Nettle is a resourceful fox who takes confusing instructions literally. Hush is an inconveniently quiet owl. Tock is an inventor whose errors are sometimes edible. Bramble is a small dragon acting imposing because he worries about a friend. Lyra stayed voluntarily to protect the fragment.

Each chapter awards one deterministic decoration: glow lantern, star rug, crystal trophy, book stack, moss habitat, sky wallpaper. Earned coins can also buy home decorations. Replays provide practice without repeated story rewards. The friendly boss chapter combines conversation, listening, sentence assembly, observation, deduction and narrative sequencing, without a timer.

## Curriculum and evidence
Track vocabulary, reading, listening, grammar, communication and writing separately. Sentence/story assembly measures supported construction, not independent free writing. Difficulty belongs to content, not school grade. Adjust complexity after consistent independent success; errors reveal hints and then stronger support without blocking story progress. Record supported completion separately from independent mastery. Schedule missed and familiar content for later retrieval using local timestamps. Target 75–85% independent success after supervised playtesting; this is a target, not a measured result.

Every authored challenge includes objective, skill, difficulty, English prompt, bilingual help/hint/explanation, answer sets and a story purpose. Stable option IDs avoid evaluating display labels. Ordered construction tokens allow alternative valid sequences. Two dialogue challenges explicitly accept two appropriate answers. Validate IDs, chapters, available answers, token multisets and nonempty translations.

If listening words are revealed or voice is unavailable, permit completion but do not claim independent listening mastery. Parents need actionable next steps and approximate active time, not inflated scores. Human curriculum review and child playtests remain necessary.

## Architecture and MVP boundaries
React/TypeScript own accessible menus and overlays; Phaser owns interactive world scenery and feedback. Zustand coordinates session state. A storage service encapsulates Dexie/IndexedDB and versioned migrations. Lightweight settings use localStorage. i18next owns interface strings while data-driven content separates bilingual support from English learning text. Zod validates saves and content. Vite builds static assets; a service worker caches local resources. No backend, authentication, analytics, API keys or external account dependencies.

Multiple local profiles isolate siblings. Meaningful changes autosave. Schema-validated JSON exports provide portable backups; imports must reject invalid data without silently overwriting unrelated profiles. Deletion/reset requires confirmation. Browser data can be cleared and is not a permanent backup.

MVP: complete World 1, three starters, six reusable challenge kinds, 36 authored challenges, mixed-skill friendly boss, progression, inventory/home, language switching and parent view. Later worlds, advanced free writing, speech recognition, production voice recordings and supervised child testing are outside this initial implementation. Speech synthesis is an optional device-dependent fallback.

## Acceptance checks
Complete onboarding in both languages and finish all chapters while making deliberate mistakes. Verify alternate dialogue answers, tile order, replay reward rules, reload, sibling isolation, backup roundtrip and invalid imports. Inspect RTL/mobile screenshots against the bar. Check keyboard/touch alternatives, reduced motion, mute and missing speech. Test production offline after caching. Real child engagement requires supervised testing with parental consent and cannot be claimed from automated tests.
