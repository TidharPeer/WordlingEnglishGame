# Encounter framing critique, round 9

**Visual direction:** This pass is a clear improvement over round 8. The forest now fills the encounter panel, the three objects sit on a shared grassy foreground, and the guide, clue, and help control float over the scene. The 320 and 375 px captures no longer end in a white strip; the surrounding forest reaches the bottom of the viewport. The success crop also makes the apple-to-sign path and the `APPLE` reveal easier to read, with a modest lift in lighting across the ground.

**Biggest remaining gap:** The reference (`screenshots/image (10).png`) is a full-frame, colorful game world centered on characters interacting on one platform. The current screen is still a quiet, dark forest tableau centered on three isolated answer objects. On desktop, the two large cream overlays occupy most of the panel's upper band, and the rounded 1100 × 552 px panel remains visibly inset within a dark 1280 × 800 viewport. On mobile, the overlays take roughly the top quarter of the 585 px stage. More environmental response and character participation would close this gap more than further polishing the sign or button art. The existing success trail and brighter terrain help, but the visible payoff is still largely confined to the apple and sign.

**Tradeoff from the earlier card framing:** The new composition restores a continuous forest, but loses the old card's clean division between reading and play. In the 320 px capture, the guide and clue cover the upper scene from about y=87 to y=241, while the creature and sign begin immediately below them. The result leaves a compressed band for the story beat above the answer objects. The overlays remain readable, though their size and cream fill still make them the strongest elements in the scene.

**Mobile layout and accessibility:**

- At 320 px, the Hebrew guide copy is reduced to 12 px and the quest/progress text at the top appears smaller still. This is the clearest text-size problem; the main English clue remains legible at 21 px. The 375 px guide uses 13 px, which is better but still small for primary guidance.
- The 320 px answer targets measure 78 × 78 px, with no measured overlaps, and the audio target is 44 × 44 px. Touch geometry is adequate. The leaf/plant option, however, blends into similarly colored foliage and is less recognizable than the apple and gold key at this scale. The dark `? ? ?` sign is also hard to parse against its brown face.
- The stage ends near y=660 in an 812 px mobile viewport. The remaining ~152 px stays forest colored, fixing the previous white break, but it is mostly unused space beneath a visible rounded panel edge. Extending or visually blending the stage into this area would make the encounter feel closer to the reference's edge-to-edge world.

**Evidence limits:** `node scripts/inspect-challenge-preview.mjs` found no target overlaps or horizontal overflow at 320, 375, 600, 768, or 1280 px. It measured a 296 × 585 px stage at 320 and a 351 × 585 px stage at 375. The success image is a cropped stage composition, not a complete success screen. These previews render source with mocked hooks and Phaser, so they establish static visual layout only; they do not verify live interaction, animation timing, focus behavior, or the next action.
