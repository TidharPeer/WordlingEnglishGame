# Decoder critique, round 11

**Biggest remaining gap:** `woods-06` now has a distinct *action*, but it is still a one-off exception rather than an established decoder game. The child opens two sentence scraps, identifies the book from three labeled objects, and places it on the sign. That is more like repairing a prop than tapping an answer row, yet the other decoder encounters still use the three-row `answer-scene`, while dialogue/listening share that selection pattern and forge/story share word tiles. The larger goal of four recognizable mini-game mechanics across the adventure is therefore still unmet. Also, the sign spells the generic `WORDS` on success; the chapter's meaningful direction, `FOLLOW THE BLUE FEATHERS`, arrives later in prose. The local payoff is visible, but the repaired object does not itself reveal the story clue.

**What improved:** In both 320 and 375 px opening captures, the blank sign and chair/book/berry are together in a woodland glade. The unlocked 375 px capture shows the English clue sentences above those props, followed by an instruction to choose an object and tap the sign. This gives the sign a clear target role. In both success captures, the book moves from the prop row to the sign, letters appear on its board, and a Continue button plus XP/coins appear on the same screen. The 800 ms delay before the feedback panel should leave a moment for the letter animation, although the static captures cannot verify its timing. Compared with the earlier `i2`/`i4` answer rows and `i3`/`i11` tile slot, `i5` now asks for a different sequence: reveal, select, place. It is still presented inside the same large cream challenge card, with Puck as a portrait. The supplied `image (10).png` reference remains more of a shared, layered world with characters and props inhabiting one play space.

**Input and retry:** Source supports a keyboard and touch route: clue buttons can be activated, prop buttons selected, and the sign button activated to place the selection. Desktop drag/drop is an extra path; a child on touch does not need to drag. Props are disabled until both clues are open. A wrong placement clears the selection, triggers the existing hint/retry feedback, and allows another try. After two errors, the book prop gets a glow. The sign has an `aria-label`, and the selected prop uses `aria-pressed`. A small accessibility defect remains: the sign uses `aria-disabled` rather than native `disabled`, so it stays in the tab order even before a prop has been selected and after success, when activation has no effect. The unselected sign's label also says to tap it to place a selected object even when no object is selected. A separate functional risk is that `answer()` silently catches persistence errors; a failed attempt or completion can leave the player without an explanation or a reliable next step.

**Learning validity:** The clue, answer, and explanation align: a book has pages and can be read. Opening both scraps requires exposure to the English sentence, but does not establish that the child read it; the illustrated and labeled book remains a strong cue, and the second scrap is only the question. This is a valid introductory function-to-object recognition exercise, with a concrete object placement reward. It is not yet much of a decoding puzzle. To deepen this encounter without expanding scope, put a short story-bearing word or direction on the repaired sign and make that reveal the reason the book mattered. For the broader game, carry the select-and-place mechanic into other decoder encounters with clues that require using the English rather than merely identifying an object picture.

**Evidence and limits:** This assessment uses the five round 11 `i5` source-rendered captures, the earlier `i2`/`i3`/`i4`/`i11` captures, `screenshots/image (10).png`, `ChallengeView.tsx`, `content.ts`, `i18n.ts`, and the decoder rules in `styles.css`. The captures mock React hooks. They verify presentation states, not live timing, audio, pointer dragging, keyboard focus order, retry transitions, or saved progression; those behavior claims above come from source inspection and need runtime validation.

**Targeted retest after changes:** The refreshed 320 and 375 px success captures replace `WORDS` with the readable, two-line `FOLLOW THE / BLUE FEATHERS`; the story direction is now visibly earned on the sign itself, and the Continue/reward panel still fits below it. Source now sets `tabIndex=-1` until clues and a prop selection make the sign actionable, and again after success. The unselected sign also gets a localized “select an object first” label. These changes resolve the story-payoff and idle keyboard-tab issues noted above. One new regression risk remains in CSS: the reduced-motion rule sets opacity on `.decoder-sign-word>span`, but the newly animated letters are grandchildren (`.decoder-sign-line>span`) with `opacity:0`. If their animation is suppressed by the app's `.reduce-motion` setting, the trail message can remain invisible. The reduced-motion selector should reveal those letter spans explicitly. This is a source-based finding; the refreshed captures do not exercise reduced motion.

The added `.reduce-motion .decoder-sign-line>span{opacity:1!important}` rule now reveals the letters when the app-wide `.reduce-motion *` rule suppresses their animation, resolving that specific regression risk in source.
