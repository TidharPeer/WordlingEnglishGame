# Mini-game critique, round 11

**Biggest gap:** The six named challenge kinds do not yet feel like four meaningfully different mini-games within an adventure. In these 375 × 812 captures, dialogue (`i2`), listening (`i4`), and decoder (`i5`) all culminate in tapping one of three stacked answer rows. Listening adds a speech control; decoder makes the child open clue buttons before those rows become available. Forge (`i3`) and story (`i11`) both use the same word-bank-to-slot interaction and Check button. The hunt has a more spatial forest scene, but still resolves by selecting one of three objects. The labels and curriculum vary more than the child's actions do.

The adventure gap is visible alongside that repetition. The reference, `screenshots/image (10).png`, puts characters and usable props together in a layered woodland play space with a light HUD. Here, Puck stays a small portrait at the top of a cream card while most of the screen is prompt, options, and feedback. The story says the sign needs words, yet the `i5` decoder presents two hidden text scraps and a disabled chair/book/berry list; the child does not handle the sign or see a place where the book belongs. `i11` asks for a picnic story, but the already labeled “First,” “Then,” and “Finally” strips make the sequence a familiar tile sort inside the same card. Chapter prose and rewards provide a narrative frame, but these particular screens rarely let the child *do* the story action.

**Smallest independently buildable improvement:** Pilot a physical decoder for `woods-06` only. Show the blank sign as the target and the existing chair, book, and berry as objects in its scene. Keep the two clue scraps, then let the child place an object on the sign by tap-select/tap-target or drag and drop. A wrong object returns to its spot with the existing hint; placing the book makes letters appear on the sign before Continue appears. This is one authored encounter and one decoder-specific interaction, so it can be built and judged without revising all 36 challenges. The immediate kid-facing payoff is that reading “something you can read” helps repair the very sign the chapter asks them to save. It also gives decoder an action distinct from the current answer-row selection, though the wider four-mechanic and adventure goals still need later passes.

**Evidence and limits:** The source supports the interaction comparison: `ChallengeView.tsx` routes dialogue/listen/decoder through the same `answer-scene` option buttons; forge/story share `placed`, `word-bank`, and `sentence-slot`; decoder gates the options on every `cluePart` being revealed. `content.ts` supplies the `woods-06` sign/book clue and the `woods-12` picnic sequence. The five screenshots verify the static mobile presentation, and the reference supplies the visual bar. These previews mock hooks, and the full runtime was unavailable, so they do not establish live audio quality, drag behavior, transition timing, saved progression, or how a completed scene actually responds.
